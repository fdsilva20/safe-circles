# Safeguarding protocol

Safe Circles is a peer listening space, not a crisis service. This protocol tells you what to do when something comes up that is beyond the circle. Follow it every time; do not improvise.

## Principles
1. **Safety over confidentiality.** The group guidelines promise confidentiality, with one exception, stated in guideline 9: when someone is in danger. Be honest with the person about this.
2. **Do no harm.** Do not ask for details of abuse or violence. Do not promise outcomes. Do not contact anyone on a participant's behalf without their agreement, except in an emergency.
3. **Adults only.** The platform and open circles are for people aged 18 and above. If you learn that a participant is under 18, tell them kindly that this circle is for adults, point them to the youth services in the referral directory, and inform Red Dot Foundation's safeguarding lead the same day.
4. **You are not alone.** Red Dot Foundation's safeguarding lead is there for you. Escalate early.

## Immediate danger
Someone describes a plan to end their life, an ongoing assault, or a threat to their life or another person's **today**.
- Stay with the person. Do not leave them alone.
- Call **112** (national emergency number). For a mental health emergency you can also call Tele-MANAS on **14416**.
- Inform Red Dot Foundation's safeguarding lead as soon as the person is safe.
- Record on the platform only that a concern was raised and escalated. No details, no names.

## Serious concern, not immediate
Someone discloses past or ongoing violence, abuse, self-harm, or severe distress, but is not in danger right now.
- After the session, speak with them privately. Thank them. Ask what support they would like.
- Offer the referral directory and help them choose. Offer to sit with them while they make a first call, if they want that.
- With their agreement, inform Red Dot Foundation's safeguarding lead within 24 hours so follow-up support can be arranged. If they do not agree, still seek anonymous advice from the safeguarding lead about what to do.
- File a concern on the platform describing the situation without identifying details.

## Concern about the circle itself
A participant or facilitator breaks the guidelines in a way that harms others: harassment, discrimination, pressuring someone to share, recording, or contact outside the circle that is unwanted.
- Stop the behaviour in the session, naming the guideline.
- If it continues, ask the person to leave the session.
- Report it through "Report a concern" the same day. Red Dot Foundation decides whether the person may return.

## Your own wellbeing
Facilitators hear hard things. Debrief with another facilitator or the safeguarding lead after any session that affected you. Do not run a session when you are not well enough to hold it; cancel it on the platform instead.

## Contacts

- Tele-MANAS (mental health, 24x7, free): **14416**
- Women's helpline: **181**
- Red Dot Foundation safeguarding lead: via the facilitator support contact given at training
