# Weekly discussion topics

Each topic gives you an opening question, three deeper questions and a closing round. Use one topic per session. Go only as deep as the group wants to; the questions are invitations, not a checklist.

## Who listens to me?

**Why this topic.** Many of us have no one who simply listens. Naming that is the first step.

**Opening question.** Think of a time someone really listened to you. What did they do?

**Deeper questions.**
- What makes it hard to be listened to at home, at work or at college?
- What do you do when you need to be heard and no one is there?
- What would change for you if you had one person who listened every week?

**Closing round.** One word for how you feel right now.

**Facilitator notes.** Keep it light for a first session. Model listening yourself. If someone shares a painful story, thank them, pause, and move on only when they are ready.

## Saying no

**Why this topic.** Saying no, to family, partners, friends or employers, is hard in most of our communities, and especially for women.

**Opening question.** When was the last time you wanted to say no and said yes instead?

**Deeper questions.**
- What happens in your body when you want to say no?
- Who in your life accepts your no? Who does not?
- What is one small no you could try this week?

**Closing round.** One small no you want to practise.

**Facilitator notes.** Do not push anyone towards a confrontation at home. A "small no" can be as small as not answering a message at once.

## Safe and unsafe places

**Why this topic.** Safety is physical and emotional. Talking about where we feel safe helps us notice where we do not, and why.

**Opening question.** Describe a place where you feel completely safe. What makes it safe?

**Deeper questions.**
- Where in your daily route do you feel least safe? What do you do about it?
- Who makes a place feel safe, and who makes it feel unsafe?
- What would make your neighbourhood feel safer for everyone?

**Closing round.** One place you will try to make a little safer for someone else.

**Facilitator notes.** Participants may name harassment or violence. Listen, do not ask for details, and remind the group of guideline 9. Mention the referral directory at the end, for anyone who wants it.

## What I was told a girl should be

**Why this topic.** The rules we were taught about gender shape how we treat ourselves and others. Examining them together loosens their hold.

**Opening question.** Finish the sentence you heard growing up: "A good girl..." or "A real man..."

**Deeper questions.**
- Which of those rules do you still follow without thinking?
- Which have you broken, and what happened?
- What would you tell a younger person in your family about these rules?

**Closing round.** One rule you are ready to let go of.

**Facilitator notes.** Men and women may be in the room. Keep the focus on each person's own experience, not on blaming any group.

## When I feel low

**Why this topic.** Low moods are common and rarely talked about. Hearing that others feel it too reduces isolation.

**Opening question.** What does a low day look like for you?

**Deeper questions.**
- What helps, even a little, on those days?
- Who notices when you are low? Who would you like to notice?
- What stops you from asking for help?

**Closing round.** One thing you will do for yourself this week.

**Facilitator notes.** This topic can surface thoughts of self-harm. Follow the safeguarding protocol: speak to the person privately after the circle, share the helpline numbers, and escalate if there is immediate risk. Do not try to counsel.

## Friendship and solidarity

**Why this topic.** Circles work because of solidarity. This session names what we give each other.

**Opening question.** Who has shown up for you when it mattered?

**Deeper questions.**
- What does being a good friend mean to you now, compared with five years ago?
- How do you show up for others? How would you like to?
- What could this circle do together outside our sessions?

**Closing round.** A thank-you to someone in or outside the room.

**Facilitator notes.** A natural session for planning a shared activity from the arts-based activities list.
