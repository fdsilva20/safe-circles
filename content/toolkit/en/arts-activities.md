# Arts-based activities

Creative activities help people explore consent, gender stereotypes, healthy relationships and wellbeing without having to talk directly about their own lives. Each activity needs 30 to 45 minutes and cheap materials.

## Story in a circle
Each person adds one sentence to a story about a character facing a hard choice (for example a young woman deciding whether to travel alone for work). Afterwards, discuss: what did the character need? Who helped?

## Body map
On a large sheet, one volunteer lies down and the group traces an outline. Everyone writes or draws, on the outline, where they feel stress, where they feel strength, and what helps. Discuss the patterns. No one is asked to point to their own body.

## Image theatre
In pairs, create a frozen "statue" of a situation without safety, then reshape it into a statue of a situation with safety. The group names what changed. Based on Boal's Theatre of the Oppressed.

## Letter to my younger self
Ten minutes of silent writing: a letter to yourself at fifteen. Nobody has to read theirs aloud. Those who wish may share one line.

## Collage of a safe city
Using newspapers and magazines, make a collage of what a safe neighbourhood looks like. Present it to the group. Connects to Safecity's mapping of public spaces.

## Rangoli of feelings
Make a shared rangoli or drawing where each colour stands for a feeling. Each person adds their colours for the week. Photograph only the artwork, never the people, and only with everyone's agreement.

## Song and proverb swap
Share a song, lullaby or proverb from your family and what it taught you about being a woman or a man. Discuss which teachings to keep and which to question.

Facilitator notes: introduce each activity as an invitation. Anyone may watch instead of taking part. Allow time to close the activity before the closing round.
